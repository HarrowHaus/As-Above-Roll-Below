# Legacy prototype art direction

This document preserves the original visual exploration for the first mechanics demo. It is historical reference only. The canonical production direction will be developed in `docs/ART_BIBLE.md`.

## Original visual sentence

**A forbidden late-1990s RPG strategy guide got photocopied through a tabletop zine, annotated by a rules lawyer, then turned into a very polished modern game UI.**

The production quality should be high. The subject matter should be stupid. That contrast is the joke.

## Original prototype targets

- warm dirty-paper surfaces rather than generic fantasy brown;
- hard black ink, thick rules, stamp-red accents, cheap-print gold;
- oversized tabloid headings instead of card-game chrome;
- monster illustrations treated like manual art rather than collectible-card frames;
- readable mobile-first controls;
- gear presented as accumulated marginalia rather than a conventional inventory grid.

The simple SVG monsters in the prototype were layout placeholders, not target final art.

## Original illustration direction

- aggressive black contour;
- crosshatching / stipple / dry brush;
- controlled photocopy breakup;
- serious heroic anatomy applied to unserious subjects;
- flat spot accents used sparingly;
- no Munchkin visual mimicry;
- no generic AI-fantasy airbrush look.

A good monster image should look legitimate for one full second before the joke registers.

## Historical production-tool shortlist

- Krita for painted/ink art and texture work;
- Inkscape for logo, vector UI, borders, and cleanup;
- LibreSprite only for deliberate pixel/CRT micro-elements;
- Kenney CC0 for temporary development primitives;
- Game-Icons for temporary icon vocabulary where attribution is satisfied.

The current Art Bible is expected to move beyond this initial zine/manual concept into a more exact production system with locked player model sheets, monster sheets, dice language, UI hierarchy, and repeatable generated-asset QA.
