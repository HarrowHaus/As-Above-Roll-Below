# Floor I Motif Candidates

**Stage:** B — world/content architecture  
**Purpose:** choose a coherent conceptual motif for the vertical-slice Floor before D-stage encounter authoring.

A Floor motif is not a theme park label. It is the **function connecting otherwise different manifestations**.

The first Floor must accomplish four things simultaneously:

1. teach contested rolls and visible Instinct;
2. establish that the Below contains both familiar and researched material;
3. introduce original Below ecology early enough that the game is not a reference museum;
4. create a boss premise strong enough to sell the setting in one vertical slice.

---

# Candidate A — THRESHOLDS

## Core question
**Who gets through, and what counts as permission?**

## Why it belongs at the entrance
The first Floor is literally the player's first sustained passage into the Below. Threshold imagery therefore needs almost no exposition.

It also connects extremely different content domains without feeling random:

- doors / gates / keys from fantasy;
- window-and-door encounter folklore;
- talking boards as attempted communication across a boundary;
- checkpoint / authorization bureaucracy;
- crossroads / liminal folklore;
- original Below gatekeepers and Indexers;
- objects whose public identity differs depending on which side of a boundary is doing the naming.

## Mechanical vocabulary
- LOCK
- permission / denial
- dice becoming eligible or ineligible
- passage conditions
- swapping which side a die belongs to
- sealing / opening one option

## Researched material already compatible
- **REF-0003 Talking Board / Ouija** — mediated contact / communication boundary.
- **REF-0006 Kelly-Hopkinsville** — repeated window/door/roofline appearance folklore.
- **REF-0004 Project STARGATE / remote viewing** can appear as an Artifact or Event rather than a creature: attempting to perceive beyond immediate boundaries.

## Original Below content
Excellent fit for:
- Indexers;
- a Native gatekeeper;
- unauthorized passages;
- false doors;
- a Convergence assembled from several gate/permission traditions.

## Tone
Mystery first, conspiracy literacy second.

This is desirable for Floor I.

## Risk
Threshold symbolism is common fantasy material and can become generic unless the writing/mechanics are precise.

## Vertical-slice strength
**Very high.**

# Candidate B — WATCHERS

## Core question
**What changes because something noticed you?**

## Content bridges
- fantasy sentries / gargoyles;
- Mothman and warning-observer interpretations;
- Men in Black folklore;
- surveillance infrastructure;
- remote viewing;
- eyes / optical devices;
- original Natives that only act when observed or unobserved.

## Mechanical vocabulary
- reveal enemy dice;
- preview future rolls;
- triggers when certain faces are visible;
- effects depending on whether a die is inspected/selected;
- enemies that change after the player interacts with them.

## Researched material already compatible
- REF-0002 Mothman
- REF-0004 STARGATE / remote viewing
- future Men in Black research

## Original Below content
Strong fit for Natives that react to observation and information.

## Risk
Can drift quickly into generic `all-seeing eye / surveillance / THEY ARE WATCHING` conspiracy aesthetics, which we specifically want to avoid.

## Vertical-slice strength
**High, but better after the game's tone is already understood.**

# Candidate C — DOUBLES

## Core question
**When two versions resemble one another, which one is the copy?**

## Content bridges
- mimics / changelings / doppelgängers;
- conspiracy shapeshifter lore;
- counterfeit documents;
- mirrored occult symbolism;
- doubles in dice mechanics;
- original Native **Doubles** from the ecology taxonomy.

## Mechanical vocabulary
- COPY
- matching pairs
- doubles bonuses;
- mirrored dice;
- enemy copies Gear / Instinct;
- exact-value duplication.

## Original Below content
Extremely strong. The entire motif can teach the game's native ecology.

## Risk
The dice word `doubles` may dominate too obviously, and modern shapeshifter conspiracy material often attaches to living-person accusations we should not touch.

## Vertical-slice strength
**Very high mechanically; stronger as Floor II once the player already understands what a normal Residue looks like.**

# Candidate D — CLASSIFICATION

## Core question
**What happens when the category becomes more powerful than the thing being categorized?**

## Content bridges
- bestiaries;
- Blue Book case classification;
- archival language;
- occult taxonomies;
- fantasy guild bureaucracy;
- misidentification folklore;
- original Indexers / Redactors;
- the game's internal provenance obsession reflected fictionally without exposing the real research apparatus.

## Mechanical vocabulary
- enemy tags change effects;
- hidden / revealed information;
- reclassifying a die;
- a monster whose Instinct depends on its current category;
- route changes caused by filing errors.

## Researched material already compatible
- REF-0007 Project BLUE BOOK
- REF-0004 STARGATE paperwork / tasking
- later historical bestiary/taxonomy research

## Original Below content
Probably the strongest home for Indexers and Redactors.

## Risk
Starting here could make the game feel like office satire / anomaly-bureaucracy fiction before the player has experienced the Below as a place.

## Vertical-slice strength
**High later. Not preferred for Floor I.**

# Recommendation — FLOOR I: THRESHOLDS

**THRESHOLDS is the current preferred Floor I motif.**

Not because it has the most conspiracy references. It has the opposite advantage: it lets the game begin as an unusually coherent fantasy dungeon and then reveal its deeper research literacy naturally.

A player can understand `door`, `gate`, `key`, `permission`, `window`, and `crossing` before knowing any lore.

The motif also mirrors the player's mechanical education:

- a rolled die is open;
- a locked die has crossed from possibility into commitment;
- two outcomes are spent crossing the combat threshold;
- two remain available as Spoils;
- the Floor itself is the threshold between Above and the broader Below.

This is unusually good alignment among **world, mechanics, level position, and title**.

# Floor I content architecture — provisional

Do not treat this as D-stage final content yet.

A healthy vertical-slice mix might contain:

## Familiar anchors
- 2–3 original/traditional fantasy enemies whose mechanics teach basic Instincts.

## Researched Residues
- 1–2 folklore-derived manifestations;
- 1 researched Artifact / Event rather than forcing every reference into monster form.

## Original Below material
- 2–3 Natives / Convergences tied to passage, authorization, windows, doors, keys, or local settlement.

## Elite layer
- one researched-or-Convergence elite;
- one strongly original elite.

## Boss
Prefer an **original Convergence or Native**, not the most famous reference on the Floor.

The first boss should establish that the Below itself is the star of the game.

---

# Research queue created by THRESHOLDS

Before D-stage authoring, verify candidates useful to this motif:

1. crossroads / threshold folklore with culturally appropriate scope;
2. historical keys / warding / gate symbolism only where mechanically worthwhile;
3. Men in Black only if it serves a boundary/visitor function without becoming generic UFO parody;
4. Flatwoods or another window/approach encounter only if its source record is stronger than a generic cryptid cameo;
5. historical bestiary / liminal-monster material that is public-domain and visually useful.

Also create at least three completely original threshold manifestations before choosing the eight normal Floor I enemies.

---

# B-stage gate for Floor I

Floor I can move from motif planning into D-stage content authoring when:

- THRESHOLDS survives art-direction review;
- at least 5 compatible researched records are verified;
- at least 5 original Below manifestations are concepted;
- the boss is original enough to establish franchise identity;
- the eight-enemy roster can teach mechanics in a deliberate order rather than simply display references.
