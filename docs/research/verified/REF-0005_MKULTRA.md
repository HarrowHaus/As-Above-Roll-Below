# REF-0005 — Project MKULTRA

**Status:** verified sensitive-history substrate. **Not approved as comedy content.**

## Canonical subject
**Project MKULTRA**, a CIA research program concerning behavioral modification and related techniques.

## Domain
Intelligence history / human experimentation / behavioral research.

## Provenance tags
- DOCUMENTED
- SENSITIVE HISTORY
- CLAIMED only for later allegations not independently established by the documentary record

## Rights / sensitivity
This subject involves real human experimentation, consent failures, drugs, institutional secrecy, and documented harm. Victims are not game-comedy material.

If used at all, the satirical target should be bureaucracy, euphemism, destroyed records, institutional arrogance, or the mechanics of classification—not the people subjected to experimentation.

---

## What is documented

The U.S. Senate Select Committee on Intelligence and the Subcommittee on Health and Scientific Research held a joint hearing on August 3, 1977 titled **Project MKULTRA, the CIA's Program of Research in Behavioral Modification**.

The hearing record and CIA material establish MKULTRA as a real CIA program and discuss research involving drugs and behavioral modification, questions of informed consent, funding mechanisms, records, and later efforts to reconstruct the scope of the program.

The 1977 hearing is the controlling public record for basic game-research purposes.

## What requires separate verification

`MKULTRA` has become an enormous umbrella term in popular conspiracy culture. Claims involving specific celebrities, schools, entertainment productions, secret continuing programs, ritual systems, or any later event are **not** made true by the documented existence of MKULTRA.

Every such claim would require its own source record and should usually remain outside this game.

## Common errors to avoid

- Do not use `MKULTRA` as a synonym for every alleged mind-control event.
- Do not attribute every CIA behavioral project to MKULTRA without project-specific evidence.
- Do not turn documented victims into character classes, monsters, or punchlines.
- Do not use later conspiracy literature as if it were part of the 1977 Senate findings.

---

## Possible game use — highly restricted

If editorially justified, the subject may inform **institutional artifacts or environmental language**, for example:

### DESTROYED FILE
A mechanic involving lost information, redaction, or unavailable records can satirize institutional documentation without joking about experimentation itself.

### SUBPROJECT
A procedural naming structure can inspire original fictional bureaucracy in the Below without claiming a real subproject existed.

### CONSENT FORM
Only as critique of institutional process and only if handled without victim humor.

Direct monster adaptation is currently **not recommended**.
