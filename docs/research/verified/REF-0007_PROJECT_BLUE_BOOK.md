# REF-0007 — Project BLUE BOOK

**Status:** verified documented-program substrate.

## Canonical subject
**Project BLUE BOOK**, the United States Air Force program that investigated reports of unidentified flying objects.

## Domain
Military / intelligence-adjacent history / UFO investigation / archival records.

## Provenance tags
- DOCUMENTED
- HISTORICAL INTERPRETATION for individual case conclusions
- CLAIMED for witness reports contained in case files

## Rights / sensitivity
Government records are strong factual substrate. Individual photographs and third-party works within later publications may have separate rights.

---

## What is documented

The National Archives preserves Project BLUE BOOK administrative records, case files, photographs, motion pictures, and related material.

NARA's federal-record guide describes the Air Force UFO-investigation lineage beginning with **Project Sign** in late 1947 and later operating as Project BLUE BOOK. The Air Force terminated BLUE BOOK in 1969.

The National Archives reproduces an Air Force fact sheet stating that 12,618 sightings were reported to Project BLUE BOOK from 1947–1969 and that 701 remained categorized as `Unidentified` when the project closed.

The same fact sheet states the Air Force's official conclusions: it found no evidence that investigated UFO reports represented a national-security threat, technology beyond contemporary scientific knowledge, or extraterrestrial vehicles.

## What this does not establish

`Unidentified` is not synonymous with `alien spacecraft`.

The existence of a case file proves that a report was received/investigated, not that the reported object existed exactly as described.

Project BLUE BOOK also should not be used as a catch-all label for every U.S. government UFO/UAP activity before or after its operating period.

## Common errors to avoid

- Do not state `701 alien craft were confirmed`.
- Do not treat every Blue Book case as unresolved.
- Do not collapse Project Sign, Project Grudge, BLUE BOOK, later DoD programs, and modern UAP offices into one organization.
- Do not use a witness report as though it were the Air Force's own factual conclusion.

## Mechanical hooks

### CASE NUMBER
An encounter or Artifact can be assigned an absurdly formal case number, echoing the archival apparatus rather than claiming a specific real case.

### IDENTIFIED / UNIDENTIFIED
A mechanic can reveal some but not all information about an encounter before combat.

### FILE STATUS
An enemy or event can change state when `classified`, `closed`, or `unidentified`, using original fictional case language rather than falsifying a real Blue Book record.

## Comedy hooks

The most useful comic engine is the collision between standardized administrative case handling and impossible dungeon reports.

A clerk can process a goblin sighting with immaculate procedural seriousness.
