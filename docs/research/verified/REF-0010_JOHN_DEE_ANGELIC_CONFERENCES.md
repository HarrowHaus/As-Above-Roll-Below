# REF-0010 — John Dee's Angelic Conferences

**Status:** verified manuscript/history substrate; later occult terminology must be used with chronological precision.

## Canonical subject
John Dee's late-16th-century records of **angelic conferences**, including material transmitted with Edward Kelley.

## Domain
History of magic / early modern manuscripts / angelic communication / ceremonial-occult reception.

## Provenance tags
- DOCUMENTED manuscripts and historical people
- HISTORICAL INTERPRETATION regarding religious/occult meaning and later reception
- CLAIMED for communications attributed by participants to angels/spirits

## Rights / sensitivity
The underlying early-modern manuscripts and historical facts are appropriate research substrate. Modern editions, translations, diagrams, typography, and occult reconstructions may carry separate copyright.

---

## What is documented

The British Library catalog identifies **Sloane MS 3188** as `John Dee's conferences with angels`, recording conferences from December 22, 1581 through May 30, 1583. The catalogue describes them as records of messages delivered by angels and other spirits with the aid of a crystal ball.

The British Library identifies **Sloane MS 3189** as *Liber Mysteriorum Sextus et Sanctus* / *The Book of Enoch* / *Liber Logaeth*, transcribed by Edward Kelley in 1583. Much of the manuscript takes the form of letters and numbers arranged in 49-by-49 grids.

The same collection identifies **Sloane MS 3191** as the only surviving book of ceremonial magic in Dee's own hand and records the `forty eight angelical keys`, received at various times in 1584 at Kraków.

These manuscript records establish that Dee and Kelley engaged in and recorded a highly structured angelic-communication project. They do not establish that supernatural beings objectively authored the material.

## Terminology caution

Modern occultism often groups Dee/Kelley material under **Enochian** terminology. The manuscript catalog itself uses historical titles such as *Liber Logaeth*, `Book of Enoch`, and `angelical keys`.

When the game references the sixteenth-century record directly, prefer the manuscript's actual terminology unless a later `Enochian` usage is separately sourced and the historical layer is clear.

## Common errors to avoid

- Do not state that the British Library verifies angelic authorship; it catalogs Dee's records of claimed communications.
- Do not treat all angel magic, Christian angelology, Kabbalah, Hermeticism, and Dee's system as one interchangeable tradition.
- Do not copy a modern occult publisher's specific diagram or reconstruction without rights review.
- Do not call every letter-grid magical artifact `Enochian` merely because the word is recognizable.

## Mechanical hooks — candidates only

### GRID
A Dee-derived Artifact or Event could arrange die values in a small matrix or care about coordinate positions, using the documented letter/number-grid character of *Liber Logaeth* without reproducing a copyrighted modern edition.

### ANGELICAL KEY
`Key` language fits a THRESHOLDS Floor naturally. A researched Artifact could unlock one otherwise unavailable manipulation or room connection.

### SCRYING
An object can preview a hidden future state / next enemy roll, but should be distinguished mechanically and historically from modern remote-viewing programs.

## Comedy hooks

The deep-cut joke can come from **excessive procedural specificity**: angelic communication accompanied by tables, grids, dates, manuscripts, keys, and transcription problems rather than generic glowing-rune aesthetics.

The game should know enough to make the paperwork stranger than the parody.
