## What is documented

The West Virginia Encyclopedia places the origin of the legend near dusk on September 12, 1952. A group of local youths reportedly saw a bright object or fireball cross the sky and went with adults to investigate on a hill near Flatwoods.

The Encyclopedia records that the group reported a pulsating light and then a large man-like figure. In the account preserved there, the reported figure was roughly twelve feet tall, about four feet wide, had a red face, green clothing or body covering hanging in folds, and appeared to float toward the group.

The event became a durable part of West Virginia folklore and is now widely known as the Flatwoods or Braxton County Monster story.

## What is claimed / folklore

The creature's exact appearance, the nature of the original aerial object, whether the figure floated, its physical identity, and extraterrestrial interpretations remain part of the witness/lore layer.

Later stylized depictions often standardize the figure into a very specific spade/hood-shaped silhouette. Game art must distinguish what is actually reported in sources from what belongs to later visual culture.

## Common errors to avoid

- Do not state as fact that an extraterrestrial spacecraft landed at Flatwoods.
- Do not present one later mascot/illustration as a photographically established creature design.
- Do not merge the Flatwoods Monster automatically with every later West Virginia cryptid legend.

## Mechanical hooks — candidates only

### APPROACH
The original story is structured around people deliberately crossing from an ordinary activity into an uncertain encounter site. That makes the case useful for a THRESHOLDS Floor without forcing it into generic alien combat.

### LIGHT / REVEAL
A visible enemy die or state can remain dormant until a player action `illuminates` it.

### FLOAT
A rule can alter adjacency or which enemy dice are eligible to lock, evoking reported motion rather than simply increasing attack power.

## Comedy hooks

The subject works best when the game respects its unusually specific regional identity. `West Virginia alien thing` is weak writing; the exact date, place, and bizarre reported silhouette give the material enough personality already.
