# REF-0002 — Mothman

**Status:** verified folklore substrate; game transformation not yet approved for Floor I.

## Canonical / common name
**Mothman**

## Location / period
Point Pleasant area, West Virginia; core reported-sighting period beginning in November 1966 and extending into 1967.

## Domain
Regional folklore / cryptid culture / Forteana.

## Provenance tags
- FOLKLORE
- CLAIMED eyewitness encounters
- DOCUMENTED cultural history around the reports
- FICTIONALIZED only when transformed into game content

## Rights / sensitivity
- General folklore name/story: low copyright risk.
- Specific modern books, films, photographs, museum art, and the Point Pleasant statue remain separately protected works.
- The 1967 Silver Bridge collapse was a real fatal disaster. Do not use victims or the collapse as a cheap combat gag.

---

## What is documented

The Smithsonian Center for Folklife and Cultural Heritage places the best-known Point Pleasant encounter on November 15, 1966, when two young couples reported seeing a large red-eyed winged figure near the abandoned TNT / power-plant area and said it pursued their car.

The West Virginia Encyclopedia treats Mothman as West Virginia folklore and describes a cluster of reports centered on Point Pleasant and the former munitions area during 1966–67. It also documents the later cultural life of the legend, including books, a local statue, museum, and annual festival.

## What is claimed / folklore

Witness descriptions, later paranormal interpretations, reported electronic disturbances, UFO associations, Men in Black stories, and theories connecting Mothman to later events belong to the folklore/claim layer unless independently established.

The often-repeated idea that Mothman predicted or caused the December 1967 Silver Bridge collapse is **not** treated as established fact.

## Common errors to avoid

- Do not present the Silver Bridge disaster as something Mothman factually caused or predicted.
- Do not flatten every later winged-creature report into the original Point Pleasant case.
- Do not copy the visual design of a specific film, statue, illustration, or commercial Mothman character.

---

## Visual facts / recurring report motifs

- humanoid / man-sized winged form;
- bright or glowing red eyes in prominent accounts;
- nighttime road / industrial-remnant setting;
- pursuit / gliding behavior in the famous 1966 report;
- strong relationship to place: Point Pleasant and the TNT-area landscape.

## Mechanical hooks — candidates only

### PURSUIT
A Mothman-derived enemy could create pressure across consecutive rounds rather than simply having a high dice total.

### RED-EYE TRIGGER
A visible die face could activate a behavior, borrowing the iconic eye motif without claiming supernatural meaning in the real-world account.

### GLIDE
An Instinct that becomes more dangerous after a narrow player win could evoke the reported pursuit without using bridge-disaster lore.

## Comedy hooks

The best comedy is cultural over-seriousness: the game treats a beloved regional cryptid with full boss-level dignity while preserving exact Point Pleasant details.

Do **not** make Appalachian residents, witnesses, or disaster victims the joke.
