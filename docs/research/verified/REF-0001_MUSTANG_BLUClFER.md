## What is documented

Denver International Airport's official art page identifies the work as **Mustang**, a **32-foot cast-fiberglass sculpture** by Luis Jiménez. The airport credits the work © 2008 and describes Jiménez's style in relation to Mexican muralists, the American Southwest, sign-painting color, and his fiberglass surface treatment.

Colorado Public Radio documents the widespread local nickname **“Blucifer.”** CPR also reports that Jiménez died in 2006 after part of the sculpture came loose while he was working on it, severing an artery in his leg. His studio later completed the piece, which was installed at DIA in 2008.

CPR reports that the glowing red eyes use LED flood lights and describes them as a tribute to Jiménez's father, who owned a neon-sign shop.

## What is lore / interpretation

The sculpture is surrounded by jokes and conspiracy folklore that characterize it as demonic, cursed, ominous, or connected to broader Denver International Airport conspiracy stories.

Those interpretations are cultural material, not established facts about the artist's intent.

The game must not state that the sculpture was commissioned as an occult object, a demon, a ritual device, or evidence of an airport conspiracy unless the statement is clearly presented as in-universe fiction rather than historical fact.

---

## Common error to avoid

Do **not** refer to the real work merely as “the Denver Airport Horse” when a real-world reference is intended.

Use the actual artwork title **Mustang**, and use **Blucifer** only as the documented popular nickname.
