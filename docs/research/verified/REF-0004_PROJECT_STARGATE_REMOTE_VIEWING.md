# REF-0004 — Project STARGATE / Remote Viewing

**Status:** verified documented-program substrate.

## Canonical subject
U.S. government-funded research and operational experimentation involving **remote viewing**, later collected in CIA's declassified **STARGATE** records.

## Domain
Intelligence history / parapsychology research / declassified records.

## Provenance tags
- DOCUMENTED program/research
- HISTORICAL INTERPRETATION for program chronology and evaluation
- CLAIMED for individual remote-viewing impressions/results

## Rights / sensitivity
Government records are strong research substrate. Do not present the existence of a program as proof that paranormal perception is established fact.

---

## What is documented

CIA maintains a declassified **STARGATE** collection containing remote-viewing session reports, protocols, evaluations, correspondence, and related parapsychology material.

A CIA public-history summary states that the Agency studied what became known as **remote viewing**, that responsibility later moved among U.S. defense/intelligence organizations, and that the program was re-evaluated in the mid-1990s before being ended rather than restored.

Declassified session reports document actual operational and training tasking. Some reports explicitly describe viewer impressions as **raw intelligence data** whose interpretation and use remained the responsibility of the requester.

A 1995 operational-tasking summary in the CIA collection states that the DoD program had received more than 200 tasks from operational military organizations between 1986 and early FY1995 and describes later efforts to numerically evaluate accuracy/value.

## What is not established by program existence

The fact that government agencies funded or used remote-viewing research does not itself establish that remote viewing is a reliable paranormal capability.

CIA's public historical summary says the 1995 review found the results too unreliable, inconsistent, and sporadic to justify operational intelligence use.

## Common errors to avoid

- Do not use `the CIA had psychic spies` as a substitute for the actual multi-agency/program history.
- Do not imply every document in the STARGATE collection was created by CIA; the collection contains material from multiple U.S. government components.
- Do not use one successful-sounding session report as proof of general efficacy.
- Program names changed over time; do not collapse STARGATE, GRILL FLAME, and every earlier/later related effort into one timeless codename without checking the specific date/source.

## Mechanical hooks

### COORDINATES
A remote-viewing artifact can reveal an upcoming enemy roll, room type, or reward candidate before the player reaches it.

### RAW INTELLIGENCE
Information can be useful but imperfectly framed: e.g. reveal two possible future outcomes, only one of which is the actual target.

### TASKING
An event can allow the player to designate a target before rolling, rewarding correct prediction without asserting real-world paranormal efficacy.

These are game-fiction transformations of documented program language.

## Comedy hooks

The strongest comedy comes from exact bureaucratic seriousness around an inherently extraordinary research question: coordinate strings, tasking, evaluation forms, and raw-intelligence disclaimers applied to dungeon nonsense.

No fake CIA quotations are required.
