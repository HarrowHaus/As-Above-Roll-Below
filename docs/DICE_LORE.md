# Dice Lore — Casting in the Below

**Status:** B-stage provisional canon  
**Depends on:** `GAME_DESIGN_BIBLE.md`, `COMBAT_MODEL.md`, `REF-0008_CLEROMANCY_ASTRAGALOMANCY.md`

This document explains why dice exist **inside the fiction** of **As Above, Roll Below** without pretending the fantasy mechanic is a reconstruction of any one historical divination tradition.

---

# 1. THE PROBLEM

The Below is made of unresolved versions.

Events, objects, creatures, explanations, and identities do not always behave as though one history has fully won.

A room can contain multiple locally possible outcomes until something forces a settlement.

Experienced delvers call this condition **the open state**.

The open state is not time travel and not a multiverse lecture. It is simply how the Below behaves when too many versions of a thing remain viable at once.

---

# 2. CASTING

Delvers use **lots** to expose and settle the open state.

The standard field method is called a **cast**.

The player sees this as dice.

No consensus exists on whether a cast:

1. predicts what the Below was already going to do;
2. forces the Below to choose among available outcomes;
3. measures pressure among competing versions; or
4. works only because generations of delvers have behaved as though it works.

The field manual does not care.

It works often enough to remain mandatory procedure.

---

# 3. WHY FOUR DICE

The standard delver cast uses **four core lots**.

This is a game-system constant first and an in-world standard second.

Later lore may contain obsolete regional methods, historical casting kits, illegal modified lots, or Below-native alternatives, but the player's ordinary field kit stays four dice.

The game does not invent a fake ancient lineage claiming that one real-world culture historically used this exact four-die combat system.

---

# 4. LOCKING

A rolled die is only a visible possibility until it becomes **locked**.

Locking means committing that result to the local encounter.

Enemy Instinct describes the rule by which a Below entity settles its own side of the clash.

The player then decides which two of four visible possibilities to lock as **Fight Dice**.

Once committed, those outcomes are spent.

---

# 5. WHY FIGHT AND SPOILS COMPETE

The two dice not committed to Fight remain **unspent possibilities**.

If the player wins the clash, those unused outcomes can stabilize into Spoils.

If the player loses, they collapse with the failed local state and produce no reward.

This creates a diegetic explanation for the core rule:

> **The outcome you spend surviving cannot also be the outcome you carry home.**

A six used to smash the monster is gone.

A six preserved while winning with weaker dice remains available to become something valuable.

Greed is therefore not an arbitrary loot multiplier. It is the act of refusing to spend your strongest possible future on immediate safety.

---

# 6. MARGIN DAMAGE

When the two sides lock their outcomes, the difference between totals is the amount by which one local version overwhelms the other.

That difference becomes damage.

No separate universal attack statistic is required.

`11 vs 10` is a glancing success.

`12 vs 5` is catastrophic.

The dice already describe how decisive the settlement was.

---

# 7. MANIPULATION

Artifact effects such as BUMP, FLIP, LOCK, COPY, and TRANSMUTE are not generic wizard spells.

They represent methods, objects, rites, devices, frauds, shortcuts, technologies, and Below-native phenomena capable of interfering with settlement.

This allows a wide content range without needing multiple parallel magic systems.

A ceremonial object, declassified-looking device, fantasy ring, or completely original machine can all manipulate dice through the same mechanical language while retaining different fictional explanations.

---

# 8. ENEMY DICE

Enemies do not need to literally crouch down and play tabletop dice with the hero.

The monster's visible dice represent its **settling pressure**.

Art direction may depict this abstractly through floating lots, projected sigils, physical dice, interface overlays, environmental marks, or another consistent visual convention.

The final visual solution belongs in the Art Bible.

Mechanically, the important fact is always visible:

**the monster settles first.**

The player sees the danger before choosing how greedy to be.

---

# 9. HISTORICAL INSPIRATION

The language of casting lots has deep historical precedent, and dice/knucklebone-like objects have been used both for gaming and divination in multiple ancient contexts.

This is inspiration, not fictional proof of a secret universal system.

The research record is maintained in:

`docs/research/verified/REF-0008_CLEROMANCY_ASTRAGALOMANCY.md`

The game should be able to say internally:

> Delvers cast lots.

It should **not** say externally:

> Ancient cleromancy was secretly this game's dungeon-combat system.

---

# 10. TONE

Casting should feel old, practical, and slightly embarrassing in its simplicity.

Civilizations have built elaborate theories around it.

Field manuals have standardized it.

Artifacts worth kingdoms have been built to tamper with it.

At the end of all that institutional effort, somebody still has to roll the fucking dice.

That is exactly the tone of **As Above, Roll Below**.
