# Canonical Terminology

**Status:** B-stage working glossary

This file prevents the design from inventing three names for the same thing in different documents.

---

## ABOVE
Ordinary consensus reality / the world from which delvers enter.

`Above` does not mean `true` and `Below` does not mean `false`.

## BELOW
The residue-layer / dungeon reality in which unresolved versions, manifestations, original ecology, and accumulated material coexist.

## STRATUM / STRATA
A coherent region or depth-band of the Below.

A game Floor may represent one traversable portion of a Stratum rather than a perfect geological layer.

## MOTIF
The conceptual function tying a Floor's mixed-domain content together: thresholds, watchers, doubles, classification, failed communication, transformation, etc.

Motif is a design term and may not appear directly to players.

## MANIFESTATION
Neutral umbrella term for a creature, object, event, place, or institution-shaped thing encountered Below.

## RESIDUE
A manifestation with a reasonably traceable Above-world substrate.

## CONVERGENCE
A manifestation assembled around multiple compatible source versions / motifs rather than one clear Above-world original.

## NATIVE
A manifestation with no known Above-world precursor.

## ERRATUM / ERRATA
Provisional term for an unstable contradiction-generated manifestation. Not yet locked as final player-facing language.

## INTRUDER
Something that entered the Below from Above.

## DELVER
Provisional generic term for an intentional human explorer of the Below.

## OPEN STATE
A local Below condition in which multiple outcomes remain viable before settlement.

## CAST
The standardized use of lots/dice to expose or settle the open state.

## CORE LOTS / CORE DICE
The player's normal four dice.

Use `dice` in ordinary player-facing mechanical language unless lore context specifically benefits from `lots`.

## LOCK
Commit a rolled result to the current settlement.

In combat, the player locks exactly two Fight Dice.

## INSTINCT
The visible deterministic rule by which a monster chooses/locks its dice.

Instinct is mechanics and characterization simultaneously.

## FIGHT DICE
The two player dice committed to the clash.

## SPOILS DICE / SPOILS PAIR
The two player dice left uncommitted after Fight Dice are chosen.

They only qualify as reward when the player wins the clash under the currently selected Spoils rule.

## SETTLEMENT
The moment locked totals resolve and margin damage is applied.

Useful lore term; player UI may simply say `CLASH` if that reads better.

## MARGIN
Difference between winning and losing locked totals.

Baseline damage equals Margin.

## SPOILS
Value stabilized from unused outcomes after a successful clash; mechanically the reward system.

## ARTIFACT
Build-defining persistent item that manipulates or reinterprets dice/rules.

## GEAR
More conventional persistent equipment affecting combat/survival.

## CONTRABAND
Limited-use / consumable item enabling a tactical rule violation or intervention.

## BUMP
Increase or decrease a die by 1 within its valid face range.

## FLIP
Turn a standard d6 to its opposite face: 1↔6, 2↔5, 3↔4.

## COPY
Make one die match another visible die where permitted.

## TRANSMUTE
Replace a specified die value with another specified value.

## MARK
Give a die a temporary property recognized by another rule/effect.

## REDACTION
Do not use as a synonym for all secrecy. When used mechanically, it means a visible piece of game information has been temporarily hidden/removed by an explicit effect.

---

# Style rules

- Prefer a small stable vocabulary to synonyms.
- Do not call Spoils `loot`, `treasure`, `salvage`, and `reward` interchangeably inside rules text. `Loot` may remain casual marketing language; **Spoils** is the game-system term.
- Do not call enemy Instinct `AI`, `behavior`, `brain`, and `locking logic` interchangeably in player-facing rules.
- `Below` is capitalized when referring to the setting.
- `Above` is capitalized when referring to consensus reality as a setting concept.
- Historical research records keep the vocabulary of their sources and do not retrofit Below terminology into real history.
