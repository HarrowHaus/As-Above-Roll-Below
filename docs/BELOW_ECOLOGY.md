# The Below — Original Ecology and Content Taxonomy

**Status:** B-stage provisional canon

The Below must become a setting owned by **As Above, Roll Below**, not a museum tour through existing conspiracy and folklore references.

This document defines how original material coexists with researched material.

---

# 1. MANIFESTATIONS

A **manifestation** is anything in the Below that behaves like a creature, object, location, institution, or event.

The term is deliberately neutral. It does not claim that the thing had a literal Above-world original.

Internal design taxonomy:

## A. RESIDUES

Manifestations with a reasonably traceable Above-world substrate.

Examples might derive from:

- a folklore report;
- a public object;
- an old text;
- a documented institution;
- a historical practice;
- a stable fantasy archetype.

A Residue is **not necessarily a copy**.

The Below may exaggerate, compress, combine, or literalize characteristics associated with the source.

Research records govern what may be claimed about the Above-world substrate.

## B. CONVERGENCES

Manifestations formed where multiple compatible versions accumulate around the same function or motif.

A Convergence may have no single correct source.

Examples:

- a gatekeeper assembled from several threshold traditions;
- a messenger whose appearance contains elements of different warning legends;
- an archival monster built from redaction, filing, secrecy, and memory loss without representing one real agency.

Convergences are a major way to turn research into **original IP** rather than cameo culture.

## C. NATIVES

Manifestations with no known Above-world precursor.

Natives prove that the Below is not merely passive storage.

They may:

- feed on unresolved states;
- manipulate Casts instinctively;
- build nests from discarded versions;
- imitate Residues badly;
- cultivate useful contradictions;
- avoid stabilized areas;
- trade in Spoils.

The deeper the player goes, the more important Natives become.

## D. ERRATA

Working term for unstable manifestations produced by direct contradiction between strong versions of the same thing.

An Erratum is not `wrong information` in a moral sense. It is a local failure to settle cleanly.

Mechanical possibilities:

- changes Instinct between rounds;
- substitutes one die face for another;
- rewrites a visible rule;
- presents two mutually exclusive names/states;
- becomes more stable when the player commits a decisive margin.

`Errata` is a useful design category but not yet confirmed as final player-facing terminology.

## E. INTRUDERS

People, tools, organizations, animals, or machines that entered from Above.

Playable characters are Intruders in the most literal sense.

Not all Intruders are adventurers. Historical expeditions, commercial scavengers, cults, scholars, tourists, soldiers, frauds, and lost idiots may all have found entrances at different times.

The game does **not** need one secret global organization controlling access.

That would collapse the setting into generic anomaly-agency fiction.

---

# 2. THE BELOW IS NOT SCP

Hard rule:

**Do not build the setting around a modern secret agency that captures, numbers, and explains every anomaly.**

Institutional language can be funny and useful, but no single bureaucracy owns reality.

Different groups may produce field manuals, case systems, maps, religious interpretations, commercial guides, and contradictory taxonomies.

The player's interface may borrow the clarity of a field manual without implying one omniscient organization authored the universe.

This preserves mystery and avoids making every reference feel like a containment-file parody.

---

# 3. DELVERS

**Delver** is the provisional generic term for people who intentionally travel Below.

Different cultures and periods should have had different names for the practice.

Modern player characters may call themselves whatever suits their archetype; the game does not require a formal Delvers Guild.

Common delver knowledge includes:

- casting lots;
- reading enemy Instinct;
- recognizing an open state;
- stabilizing Spoils;
- identifying dangerous Strata;
- knowing that a familiar name does not guarantee a familiar thing.

Delvers disagree about virtually everything else.

---

# 4. SPOILS AS ECOLOGY

Spoils are not simply coins dropped by dead monsters.

A successful clash leaves unused possibilities available to stabilize.

The player captures that stability as a reward.

A Spoil may therefore become:

- an object;
- money recognized by Below traders;
- a usable Contraband effect;
- Gear;
- an Artifact;
- information;
- access;
- a temporary condition.

The exact reward economy remains governed by the Game Design Bible.

---

# 5. ORIGINAL CREATURE FAMILIES — DESIGN SEEDS

These are **families**, not final enemy names.

## MARGINALS

Small Natives that live around incomplete or weakly stabilized manifestations.

Behavioral theme:
- steal low-value dice/results;
- consume discarded Spoils;
- swarm around ties;
- flee decisive margins.

Visual direction:
- should not simply be `paper goblins`;
- art bible must find a material/biological language native to the Below.

## REDACTORS

Convergences or Natives associated with missing information rather than a specific government agency.

Behavioral theme:
- temporarily hide one die value;
- blank item text until inspected;
- remove one option from an Event;
- become weaker when the player reveals hidden information.

Important:
This category must not imply that all historical redaction is supernatural or conspiratorial.

## DOUBLES

Imitative Natives that copy stable Residues but get one important thing wrong.

Behavioral theme:
- COPY dice;
- mirror player Gear;
- reproduce the last Instinct used;
- generate excellent deep-cut comedy when an imitation misunderstands a highly specific researched object.

## INDEXERS

Working family for creatures that physically reorganize adjacency in the Below.

Behavioral theme:
- change route connections;
- move encounters between nodes;
- classify the player incorrectly and apply the consequence;
- create temporary Floor-level rules.

Avoid turning them into cute library mascots.

## NULLS

Deep-layer manifestations with almost no stable Above association.

Behavioral theme:
- fewer jokes;
- simpler but alien rule violations;
- may interact with unrolled dice or prevent prediction;
- should feel genuinely unsettling after a game full of named references.

Nulls help preserve the possibility that the Below is more than accumulated culture.

---

# 6. FLOOR ASSEMBLY

A Floor should not be assembled by domain.

Instead:

1. choose a **motif**;
2. choose the mechanical lesson;
3. draw compatible Residues from several domains;
4. add Convergences that bridge them;
5. add at least one Native that makes the setting ours;
6. ensure the boss expresses the motif rather than merely having the largest numbers.

Example motif:

## THRESHOLDS

Possible content pool:
- fantasy gatekeeper;
- reported window/door encounter folklore;
- talking-board communication artifact;
- public entrance object;
- original Indexer or Redactor;
- key/permission Gear;
- Event involving passage or refusal.

The shared function makes the variety coherent.

---

# 7. REFERENCE DENSITY

A typical Floor should not be all references.

Provisional target for a mature content pool:

- 30–40% traditional fantasy / broadly legible archetype;
- 20–30% researched folklore / weird-history / esoteric reference;
- 10–20% institutional / conspiracy-lore reference;
- 25–35% original Below material.

These ranges overlap deliberately and are not quotas.

The point is to avoid two failures:

1. `generic fantasy game with occasional conspiracy jokes`;
2. `conspiracy wiki where every enemy is a named reference`.

---

# 8. DEEP-LAYER RULE

As depth increases:

- reference density may decrease;
- original Native content increases;
- Convergences become harder to reverse-engineer;
- visual language becomes less Above-derived;
- mechanics violate assumptions more often;
- explanations become less reliable.

The deepest boss should not simply be `the biggest conspiracy`.

The game should ultimately reveal that all of the familiar references were the shallow, human-readable part of the problem.
