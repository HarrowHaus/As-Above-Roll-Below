# Floor I — Original Manifestation Seeds

**Stage:** B — original-setting development  
**Floor motif:** THRESHOLDS  
**Status:** concept seeds, not D-stage final roster

These manifestations exist to ensure Floor I belongs to **As Above, Roll Below** rather than functioning as a parade of researched cameos.

Names are working names until art, trademark/search, and D-stage writing review.

---

# 1. LATCHLING

**Taxonomy:** Native  
**Role:** early normal enemy / tutorial-level rule variation

## Concept
A small Below-native animal that lives in the seam between a thing being open and a thing being closed.

It is not shaped like a lock, key, or cute door mascot. Its anatomy should suggest that it wedges itself into narrow boundaries: flattened body, hooked limbs, pressure-adapted joints, and a silhouette that makes sense before the player knows the name.

Delvers find them in doorframes, cracked vaults, half-collapsed passages, broken cases, and occasionally between two pages that refuse to separate.

## Mechanical seed
**Instinct: TIGHT** — prefers the two enemy dice closest in value.

**Rule seed: HOLDS ON TIES** — if a clash ties, the Latchling receives a small temporary advantage next round.

Purpose:
- teaches that Instinct changes enemy personality;
- makes ties relevant without adding a new resource;
- low enough complexity for early Floor I.

## Tone
Field manuals treat infestation as a maintenance problem.

That is funnier than giving it dialogue.

# 2. TURNBACK

**Taxonomy:** Native  
**Role:** normal enemy / teaches WIDE Instinct

## Concept
A creature that forms on paths repeatedly approached and abandoned.

Its body appears oriented in two directions at once. It should not be a literal two-headed animal; anatomy, posture, footprints, or surface pattern should create the contradiction more subtly.

It follows movement toward an exit and becomes agitated when the traveler commits to continuing.

## Mechanical seed
**Instinct: WIDE** — highest + lowest.

Its attack therefore looks weak or strange until the player understands that extreme values are the point.

Possible one-line rule:
**SECOND THOUGHT:** after the player uses a manipulation, Turnback may alter which of its eligible dice are locked according to its already-visible WIDE rule.

This needs prototype testing; no hidden AI is permitted.

## Tone
It is an ecological consequence of indecision, not a motivational metaphor.

---

# 3. THE UNADMITTED

**Taxonomy:** Native or Erratum — deliberately ambiguous  
**Role:** tougher normal / unsettling early lore

## Concept
A human-shaped result of a crossing that never finished.

Not a ghost.

Not necessarily a former person.

It looks as though a human silhouette was defined from the wrong side of a boundary and never fully resolved into depth.

The important horror is not gore. It is incomplete admission.

## Mechanical seed
**Instinct: TIGHT**

**Rule: UNRESOLVED** — ties benefit it instead of producing an inert next round. Candidate effects:
- heal 1 HP on a tie; or
- gain +1 to the next locked total.

Only one version should survive D-stage balance.

## Story function
This is the first enemy that makes the player ask whether all Below creatures have an Above-world story.

Answer: no one knows.

# 4. DOORWAKE

**Taxonomy:** Native  
**Role:** normal enemy / visual surprise

## Concept
Some thresholds leave a temporary wake after something crosses them.

Most dissipate.

Sometimes the wake detaches.

A Doorwake is not a mimic pretending to be a door. The door is already behind you. This is the **afterimage of passage** moving on its own.

Its visual design should be built from displaced depth, perspective errors, trailing surfaces, and an implied rectangular absence without becoming `a door with teeth`.

## Mechanical seed
**Instinct: LOWEST** or another apparently weak lock rule.

**Rule: AFTER YOU** — the first time the player deals a large margin, Doorwake preserves one of the player's spent Fight values as part of its next roll.

This makes the player's decisive attack leave a literal mechanical wake.

## Design note
Very strong original-IP candidate if the art language succeeds.

---

# 5. TOLL EATER

**Taxonomy:** Native  
**Role:** greed-oriented normal enemy

## Concept
A scavenger that feeds on the outcomes people surrender in order to cross safely.

It gathers around well-used passages where generations of delvers have repeatedly spent strong Casts on survival.

It is not a literal coin-toll monster.

## Mechanical seed
The Toll Eater cares about **high Fight Dice**, not high Spoils.

Candidate rule:
**TOLL:** when the player commits a 6 to Fight, the Toll Eater gains a small defensive benefit this round.

The player can often avoid paying by fighting more greedily with weaker dice.

This is an elegant reversal: the enemy punishes excessive safety rather than greed.

## Purpose
Shows that monster rules can change how the core tradeoff is valued without changing the four-dice grammar.

# 6. THE SEAL-BEARER

**Taxonomy:** Convergence  
**Role:** elite

## Concept
A heavily stabilized manifestation built around the idea that passage becomes legitimate only when marked, stamped, witnessed, or sealed.

It is not a parody government employee.

Its body/equipment should combine several **original** forms of authorization into one oppressive ritual object: impressions, wax-like scars, punched apertures, knots, countersigns, and repeated marks that never correspond cleanly to a real-world agency or religion.

## Mechanical seed
**Dice pool:** elite-grade authored pressure.

**Rule: SEALED FACE** — before the player's roll, one face value is visibly declared sealed. Dice showing that value may still exist but cannot be manipulated this round.

This is easy to read and makes manipulation builds adapt without deleting them.

## Reward seed
Defeating it should plausibly unlock a passage or yield an Artifact that lets the player ignore one eligibility restriction later.

---

# 7. KEYMONGER

**Taxonomy:** Intruder-descended culture, Convergence, or unique NPC — unresolved  
**Role:** Event / merchant-adjacent character

## Concept
A trader who deals not primarily in keys, but in **permission to treat something as a key**.

They may sell:
- a physical key;
- a password nobody remembers setting;
- a stamped chit;
- the right to open one thing once;
- a convincing argument that a wall counts as a door.

The joke works only if the transaction system is mechanically legitimate.

## Mechanical seed
Event choices could exchange:
- Coins for route access;
- HP for an Artifact;
- a Gear slot for a one-Floor permission effect;
- one known future node for an unknown shortcut.

## Tone
No salesman patter.

The Keymonger behaves as though this is a mature and regulated trade.

# 8. THE FIRST DOOR

**Taxonomy:** Convergence / possibly Native — deliberately unresolved  
**Role:** preferred original Floor I boss seed

## Concept
Every culture has doors, gates, thresholds, passages, seals, guarded openings, ritual entries, forbidden rooms, borders, initiations, and stories about what happens when you cross.

The Below has had a very long time to accumulate that function.

Delvers call one manifestation **the First Door**.

Nobody credible claims it was literally the first door ever built.

Nobody has produced a better name.

It should not simply look like a large wooden door.

Visual seed:
- freestanding threshold architecture made from mutually incompatible structural eras/materials;
- no wall around it;
- openings that do not agree about perspective;
- multiple handles / closures that cannot all exist on the same side;
- native tissue/mineral/material occupying the seams rather than a monster face in the center.

The player should understand `door` immediately and then become increasingly unsure what they are looking at.

## Boss dice-engine seed
The First Door has **two readable states** determined by a separate visible Hinge die.

### CLOSED
The boss uses a safer / more defensive dice profile.

Candidate rule:
- incoming margin damage is capped at a visible amount.

The player can still qualify Spoils, but huge attacks cannot simply delete the phase.

### OPEN
The boss uses a stronger / riskier dice profile.

Candidate rule:
- additional enemy die becomes eligible;
- damage cap disappears;
- successful player Spoils receive a visible reward advantage.

This creates the Floor's central lesson at boss scale:

**When the opening appears, the reward and the danger increase together.**

The exact die pools, Hinge faces, HP thresholds, and reward modifier belong in D-stage simulation.

## Why this boss matters

- original IP rather than famous-reference boss;
- embodies THRESHOLDS;
- uses the game's open/closed vocabulary literally and mechanically;
- visually establishes that the Below generates its own manifestations;
- allows the researched references elsewhere on the Floor to enrich the setting without owning the climax.

# Floor I original-content gate

This document satisfies the B-stage requirement for at least five original threshold manifestations **as concepts**.

Before D-stage roster lock:

1. run name-conflict / trademark searches on final names;
2. reject any concept that becomes visually generic during Art Bible work;
3. test each mechanic against the four-dice simplicity rule;
4. choose no more than 4–5 of these for the actual vertical slice;
5. create additional originals if the selected researched references begin to dominate the roster.
